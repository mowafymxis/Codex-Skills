# Page patterns

Assign a purpose to every page. Combine patterns only when the hierarchy remains obvious.

## Front matter

- **Cover:** title, subtitle, owner or brand, edition/date, and one dominant visual gesture.
- **Inside cover or colophon:** authorship, edition, rights, credits, and document status.
- **Contents:** section hierarchy with stable page references; keep labels short.
- **Reader note:** purpose, scope, how to use the document, and changeability caveats.

## Section patterns

- **Section opener:** one idea, short orientation, clear section marker, and optional focal image.
- **Editorial essay:** controlled body measure, useful subheads, pull quote only when it adds navigation or meaning.
- **Feature spread:** one dominant image or diagram paired with a concise explanatory block.
- **Profile:** identity, evidence-backed description, key details, quote or verdict, and sources.
- **Gallery:** consistent crops or a deliberately composed collage with captions and credits.
- **Comparison:** shared criteria, aligned columns, visible source/assumption labels, and no invented scores.
- **Checklist or playbook:** ordered actions, ownership, decision points, and enough whitespace for scanning or annotation.
- **Reference page:** dense but structured labels, alphabetical or categorical navigation, and repeated headers.
- **Callout page:** one meaningful quote, fact, rule, or action; do not use filler slogans.

## Closing patterns

- **Summary:** synthesize rather than repeat section titles.
- **Next action:** one explicit action, contact, or decision path.
- **Sources and credits:** human-readable citations, image credits, access dates when required, and rights notes.
- **Back cover:** restrained brand mark, contact, edition, or closing statement.

## Rhythm rules

- Alternate dense and quiet pages intentionally.
- Change scale at section boundaries, not randomly inside paragraphs.
- Repeat navigation and folios consistently.
- Keep each spread to one primary focal idea.
- Do not isolate a small leftover paragraph on a new page merely to hit a page count.
